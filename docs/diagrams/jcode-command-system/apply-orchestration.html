Chapter 03 · execution topology

Rigor follows
risk.

/apply and /apply:all share one lifecycle engine. Risk and dependency shape determine direct work, swarm review, deep DAG execution, and human gates.

Approved design

01 / LEVELS

Why three over two?

Three levels preserve a useful middle: routine work does not pay frontier-review cost, while high-risk work cannot hide inside a single “agentic” bucket. The design can still collapse behavior when observable risk does not justify separation.

L1 · direct

Low-risk, bounded work. One accountable executor plus deterministic checks.

L2 · reviewed

Normal risk. Independent review when warranted, usually same-provider capable.

L3 · adversarial

High risk. Different-provider family review plus separate acceptance verification. Critical risk adds multiple frontier reviewers and a human gate.

02 / TOPOLOGY

Single feature or queue.

Diagram fallback: the scheduler classifies risk and dependency topology, then selects direct work, a light swarm, or a deep DAG. Jcode remains durable authority; Orca may own runtime resources. Every route reconverges at verification.

03 / POLICY

Pre-classify, do not fail cheap first.

if risk == "high":
    reviewer.provider_family = different_from(author.provider_family)
    acceptance_verification = required
elif risk == "normal":
    reviewer = independent_when_warranted
else:
    deterministic_checks = required

# Cross-provider review only when high (the approved default).
Orca boundary: Jcode owns selected work, approvals, schedules, idempotency, checkpoints, and settlement. Orca owns verified runtime resources. Runtime observations are evidence, not durable settlement authority.
